# The Window

@audio https://wvmjobeketinbzxggzuq.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/audio/b1-pistache-above-the-bakery/chapter_1.page_1-1792684975052.mp3
My name is Pistache. I am a ginger cat with one white sock on my left paw, and I live on the third floor of an old building in Lyon, just above a bakery. My human is called Léa. She is a baker, so she gets up when the whole city is still asleep.
Every night at three o'clock, her alarm rings. Léa groans, puts on her slippers and goes downstairs to make bread. I stay in bed for a few more minutes, because the blanket is still warm. Then I go to my favourite place in the world: the kitchen window.

---

@audio https://wvmjobeketinbzxggzuq.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/audio/b1-pistache-above-the-bakery/chapter_1.page_2-1792684981649.mp3
From the window, I can see everything. I can see the street, the rooftops and the chimneys, and far away, the lights of the city. At that time of night, the street is silent. Only the smell of fresh bread climbs up the walls and comes in through the window. It smells like butter and home.
But the window is not just a place to watch the world. It is also where I keep my secrets. For example, I know that the man in the ground-floor flat across the street never opens his curtains. His name is written on the letterbox: Albert Morel. I have seen him only twice. He is old, he walks slowly, and he always looks at the ground.

@audio https://wvmjobeketinbzxggzuq.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/audio/b1-pistache-above-the-bakery/chapter_1.page_3-1792684987234.mp3
My other secret is more exciting. Every night, a small grey cat appears on the roof opposite. She walks along the edge, between two chimneys, as if the roof belonged to her. She has no collar. Her tail is long and thin, and one of her ears has a little cut at the top.
Sometimes she stops and looks straight at me. I look back. Neither of us moves. Then she jumps onto another roof and disappears into the dark.
I have never been outside. Léa says the city is too dangerous for a cat like me. "You're an indoor cat, Pistache," she tells me, scratching my head. "You have everything you need here."
She is right, I suppose. I have a bowl that is never empty, a sofa, a sunny spot in the afternoon and a human who loves me. But when the grey cat runs across the rooftops, my paws feel strange. They feel like they want to follow her.

# The Open Window

@audio https://wvmjobeketinbzxggzuq.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/audio/b1-pistache-above-the-bakery/chapter_2.page_1-1792701072245.mp3
It happened on a Tuesday in October. The night was warm, and Léa was in a hurry. She opened the kitchen window to let some air in, drank her coffee in two seconds and ran downstairs. She forgot to close the window.
I sat on the windowsill for a long time. The air smelled of wet leaves, bread and something else, something wild. Below me, there was a narrow ledge, and from the ledge, a gutter led to the roof next door. It was not very far. Even for an indoor cat.
"Don't do it, Pistache," I told myself. And then I did it.
The ledge was cold under my paws. I walked very slowly, my belly close to the stone. When I reached the gutter, I jumped. For one terrible second, there was nothing under me. Then my claws touched the tiles, and I was on the roof. My heart was beating like a drum.

@audio https://wvmjobeketinbzxggzuq.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/audio/b1-pistache-above-the-bakery/chapter_2.page_2-1792701081246.mp3
"Not bad for a sofa cat," said a voice behind me.
I turned around. It was her, the grey cat. She was sitting next to a chimney, looking at me with her yellow eyes. Up close, she was even thinner than I thought.
"I'm not a sofa cat," I said. "My name is Pistache."
"Pistache? Like the ice cream?" She laughed, a short, dry sound. "I'm Nuage. Humans used to call me that, a long time ago. Come on, Ice Cream. I'll show you the real city."
And she did. We ran along the rooftops of the Croix-Rousse, the old hill where silk workers lived a long time ago. Nuage knew every path: which tiles moved, which windows were open, which balcony had a bowl of food for street cats. She showed me a terrace full of tomato plants, a pigeon who thought he was a king, and a place on top of a high wall where you could see the two rivers, the Rhône and the Saône, shining in the dark.
"Every night, I watch you in your window," Nuage said. "You look like a prince in a tower."
"And you look free," I answered.
She didn't say anything. She just looked at the sky. In the east, it was starting to turn pink.

# The Storm

@audio https://wvmjobeketinbzxggzuq.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/audio/b1-pistache-above-the-bakery/chapter_3.page_1-1792776043871.mp3
"I have to go home," I said. "Léa will come back up at seven to feed me."
"Then you'd better hurry," said Nuage. "The weather is changing."
She was right. The pink sky became grey, then dark purple. A cold wind came from the river and pushed the leaves along the street. Then the first drop of rain fell on my nose. A second later, the sky opened.
I had never seen rain from the outside. From my window, rain is a nice sound. On a roof, it is a waterfall. The tiles became slippery, the gutters became rivers, and every building suddenly looked the same. I turned left, then right, then left again. Where was my window? Where was the bakery? I couldn't smell the bread anymore. I could only smell water.
"Nuage!" I called. No answer. The thunder was too loud.
I found a small space under a chimney and pressed my body against the bricks. I was wet to the bone. My beautiful ginger fur looked like an old, dirty carpet. I closed my eyes and thought about my blanket, my bowl and Léa's warm hands. For the first time in my life, I was really afraid.
Then something touched my ear. It was Nuage, soaked like me.

"Follow me," she said. "And don't stop."
We went down a drainpipe, crossed a little courtyard and squeezed under a wooden door that didn't close properly. Inside, it was dry and dark. There were old boxes, a broken bicycle and a smell of dust and soap. We were in the storage room of a building.
Suddenly, a light came on. An old man stood in the doorway, wearing a brown dressing gown. He looked at us. We looked at him. I knew that face. It was Albert Morel, the man who never opens his curtains.
"Well, well," he said softly. "Two little sailors who lost their boat."
I expected him to shout or to chase us away. Instead, he went back into his flat and returned with an old towel and a plate of chicken. He put them on the floor and stepped back.
"Go on," he said. "I won't hurt you."
Nuage ate first, very fast, as if someone could take the plate away. I was too nervous to eat. But when Albert sat down on a box and started to dry my head with the towel, I began to purr. I couldn't help it.

# Lost Cat

@audio https://wvmjobeketinbzxggzuq.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/audio/b1-pistache-above-the-bakery/chapter_4.page_1-1792776055982.mp3
Albert let us sleep in his kitchen, next to the radiator. His flat was dark and very tidy. On the wall, there were photos of a smiling woman with short white hair. In one photo, she was holding a big black cat in her arms.
"That's Jeanne, my wife," Albert told us, even though we were cats and couldn't answer. "And that's Charbon. He lived with us for eighteen years." He was quiet for a moment. "The flat has been very silent since they left."
We slept for the rest of the day and the whole night. The next morning, the sky was clean and blue. Albert made coffee and opened the front door to look at the weather. That's when he saw the paper on the bakery window across the road.
He put on his glasses, crossed the street and read it. Then he came back very quickly, much faster than I thought an old man could walk.
"LOST CAT," he read aloud from his phone, where he had taken a photo of the poster. "Ginger, one white paw, answers to the name Pistache. Very gentle. Please call Léa." He looked down at me. "Answers to the name Pistache, eh?"
I meowed. It was true.
"So you're the cat from the window above the bakery," he said. "You were just across the street the whole time, you silly thing."

Albert found an old basket in a cupboard. It smelled faintly of another cat, of Charbon. He put me inside. Nuage watched from under the table. When Albert opened the front door, she ran out and jumped onto the wall of the courtyard. She didn't want to be carried. She never wanted to be carried.
The bakery was full of people. When the little bell above the door rang, Léa looked up from the counter. Her eyes were red. I could tell she had not slept at all.
Then she saw the basket. And she saw me.
"Pistache!" she cried. She ran around the counter, opened the basket and held me so tightly that I could hardly breathe. Her apron was covered in flour, and soon my fur was too. I didn't care. I pressed my head against her chin and purred as loudly as I could.
"Thank you," Léa said to Albert. "Thank you so much. Where did you find him?"
"In my storage room," he said with a small smile. "With a friend."

# Two Windows

@audio https://wvmjobeketinbzxggzuq.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/audio/b1-pistache-above-the-bakery/chapter_5.page_1-1792776067176.mp3
After that day, many things changed on our street.
The first change was small. The next morning, at seven o'clock, Albert opened his curtains. Then he opened his window. Then he came to the bakery and bought a croissant. Léa offered him a coffee and they talked for twenty minutes. The next day, he came back. And the day after that.
Soon, Albert came every morning. He told Léa about Jeanne, about the silk workers of the Croix-Rousse, about his job as a train driver forty years ago. Léa told him about her dream of opening a second bakery one day. I listened from my place on the stairs. Humans talk a lot, but sometimes what they say is interesting.
The second change was about Nuage. Every evening, Albert put a plate of food and a bowl of water in his courtyard. Every evening, the food disappeared. But Nuage never came inside. She watched Albert from the wall with her yellow eyes, and she ran away when he came too close.
"She's afraid," Albert said to Léa one morning. "Someone hurt her once, I think. Look at her ear."
"Give her time," Léa said. "Some cats need a lot of time."

So he gave her time. He talked to her every evening, softly, from the doorway. He left the door of the storage room open, with an old blanket inside. One week passed. Then two. Then, on a cold night in November, when the first frost covered the rooftops, Nuage walked through the door of Albert's flat and lay down next to the radiator. Exactly where we had slept on the night of the storm.
She never left.
The third change was about me. Léa now closes the kitchen window every time she goes downstairs. She checks it twice. I don't mind. I have been outside, and I have seen the rivers shining in the dark. I know the city is beautiful, and I know it is dangerous. Both things are true.
So now, every night at three o'clock, when Léa's alarm rings, I go to my favourite place in the world. I look at the street, the rooftops and the chimneys. And across the road, in the window of the ground-floor flat, where the curtains are always open now, a grey cat with a cut ear is sitting on the windowsill, looking back at me.
We don't move. We don't need to. We are both home.
